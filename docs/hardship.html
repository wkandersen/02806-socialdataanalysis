Socioeconomic Indicators

Lets take a moment to look at the socioeconomic state of Chicago. To measure this, we will look at the Census Data (CityOfChicago.org) collected by the city of Chicago. This dataset contains a variety of socioeconomic indicators for each of the 77 community areas in Chicago, including the Hardship Index, which is a composite measure of socioeconomic hardship based on factors such as unemployment, poverty, and education levels. The socioeconomic data provides a better understanding of how life is for the residents of Chicago, and how it may relate to crime patterns in the city. N.B. Correlation between Hardship and other indicators

Finally, we can look at the correlation between the Hardship Index and the other socioeconomic indicators across the community areas in Chicago.

Looking at the visualizations above, we can see how the various socioeconomic indicators are correlated to the hardship index. It is clear that a high unemployment rate, a high poverty rate, and a low per capita income are all associated with a high hardship index. Geographically, we can see that the areas with the highest hardship index are located in the south and west sides of Chicago, while the areas with the lowest hardship index are located in the north side, near the coast, of Chicago.
